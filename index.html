02 / THE MECHANISM

A simple
message rule.

Each member sends a fraction of their current signal along each tie. A noisy-OR combines incoming messages while preserving an individual baseline.

pᵢ (t+1)=1 − (1 − pᵢ⁰) ∏j ∈ N(i) (1 − β · wⱼᵢ · pⱼ(t))

pᵢ⁰synthetic baseline risk for member i

βthe message-strength control

wⱼᵢtie strength from member j to i

pⱼ(t)member j’s signal in the current round

This compact rule is for explanation, not inference. Real churn models need validated data, leakage controls, calibration, time-aware evaluation, governance and a clear intervention policy.

03 / THE PROFESSIONAL STORY

Built from a real question.
Shown with a new example.

At Nextel, I developed a proprietary graph-based churn model called Favorites. It drew on network influence, message-passing ideas and probabilistic graphical models to examine how churn by an influential member could change risk across their close network. I explored graph tooling including NetworkX and graph databases.

04 / RESEARCH CONTEXT

Ideas with a research trail.

These sources ground the modeling concepts and the telecom use case. They are context for this demonstration, not validation of the historical Favorites model.

FOUNDATIONS · PROBABILISTIC MODELS

Koller & Friedman · 2009

Probabilistic Graphical Models: Principles and Techniques. A comprehensive reference on model representation, inference and learning.

MIT Press ↗
FOUNDATIONS · MESSAGE PASSING

Kschischang, Frey & Loeliger · 2001

“Factor graphs and the sum-product algorithm.” A foundational treatment of local message passing for probabilistic inference. The demo’s noisy-OR update is a different, simplified rule.

IEEE Transactions on Information Theory ↗
APPLICATION · TELECOM CHURN

Verbeke et al. · 2014

“Social network analysis for customer churn prediction.” Studies relational learning and network effects in real telecom churn data.

Applied Soft Computing ↗
EVALUATION · NETWORK DESIGN

Óskarsdóttir et al. · 2017

“Social network analytics for churn prediction in telco.” Across eight call-detail datasets, network construction affected results; collective inference did not improve performance in their benchmark.

Expert Systems with Applications ↗

Predictive association does not by itself establish causal peer influence. These publications provide scientific context; they do not verify or reproduce Favorites or its recalled 0.80–0.85 ROC-AUC.
